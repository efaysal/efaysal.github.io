\end{abstract}

The “why” of non-integrability lies in number theory.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Local-to-Global Principle.} The Hasse principle states that an equation has a solution in \(\mathbb{Q}\) only if it has solutions over \(\mathbb{R}\) and all \(\mathbb{Q}_p\).
    \item \emph{Quadratic Prototype.} \(x^2 + 1 = 0\) is solvable in \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) iff \(p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}\) or \(p=2\). For \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) (including \(p=7\)), no solution exists; \(-1\) is not a quadratic residue.
    \item \emph{Brauer–Manin Obstruction.} Extending to the rational octonion algebra \(B\) (where \(B \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{O}\)), the ability to define a complex structure on \(S^6(\mathbb{Q}) \subset \mathbb{Q}^7\) depends on whether \(B \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_p\) splits or remains a division algebra:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Over \(\mathbb{R}\): \(B\) splits; \(J\) exists.
        \item \(p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}\): \(B\) is expected to split; a local complex structure exists.
        \item \textbf{\(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\), including \(p=7\)}: \(B\) remains division; \textbf{no local complex structure exists.}
    \end{itemize}
    The absence of local solutions at \(p=7\) and other \(3 \pmod{4}\) primes constitutes a Brauer–Manin obstruction, proving that no global integrable complex structure exists on \(S^6(\mathbb{Q})\). The prime \(7\) is thus intrinsic to the obstruction, providing the 7-adic topology \(\mathbb{Q}_7\) relevant to the 7-dimensional space \(\mathbb{Q}^7\) in which \(S^6(\mathbb{Q})\) is defined.
\end{itemize}

The geometric obstruction (\(N_J \neq 0\)) is a consequence of the arithmetic obstruction (Brauer–Manin).

\begin{itemize}
    \item The number-theoretic obstruction \textbf{mandates} non-associativity at the fundamental level \([v] \neq 0\).

The \(W\) and \(Z\) bosons acquire mass not as a contingent Higgs parameter but as a \emph{computable, number-theoretic inevitability}. This reinterprets the Higgs mechanism as a fundamental arithmetic-geometric constraint on the structure of the universe.

The Arithmetic of Order (AoO) is founded on the natural counting sequence 1 to \(n\) to \(n+1\), a principle I learned from my Mother (XX) and my Father (XY).}

\begin{abstract}
For over sixty years geometers have known that the unit 6-sphere, \(S^6\), admits a canonical almost–complex structure coming from the octonions—but that structure is provably non-integrable. Independently, number theorists know that the purely local obstacle to “building an \(i\)” in the rationals is the quadratic-residue test for \(x^2+1=0\) at each prime \(p\). The Arithmetic of Order (AoO) framework unites these two obstructions—one algebraic/geometric, one arithmetic—into a single, computable physical law. In AoO, what mathematicians call a frustrating “no” to an integrable complex structure on \(S^6\) becomes a predictive “yes” to nonzero electroweak mass: the primes themselves endow the \(W,Z\) bosons with mass.
\end{abstract}

\section{Finitistic Foundations and Emergent Geometry}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{No a~priori continuum.} Reality begins with rationals \(\mathbb{Q}\), generated by Farey recursion: finite, computable, yet unbounded.
    \item \emph{Octonions over \(\mathbb{Q}\).} From \(\mathbb{Q}\) one builds the 8D algebra \(\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{Q}}\) and its 7D imaginary subspace \(\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong \mathbb{Q}^7\).
    \item \emph{The rational 6-sphere.} The unit-norm locus in \(\mathbb{Q}^7\) defines \(S^6(\mathbb{Q})\). As denominators grow, \(S^6(\mathbb{Q})\) converges densely to \(S^6(\mathbb{R})\): the continuum is emergent, not assumed.
    \item \emph{Coset geometry.} The coset \(G_2/(SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R)\) is 8-dimensional over \(\mathbb{Q}\), containing a canonical copy of \(S^6(\mathbb{Q})\) that asymptotically yields the familiar real coset manifold.
\end{itemize}

\section{Geometric Obstruction: Non-Integrability via Associators}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{States and associators.} Fundamental AoO states are triples
    \[
    v = (o_1, o_2, o_3) \in \operatorname{Im}\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{Q}}^3.
    \]
    Their \emph{associator} is defined by
    \[
    [v] := (o_1 o_2) o_3 - o_1 (o_2 o_3),
    \]
    measuring intrinsic non-associativity, i.e., “internal tension.”
    \item \emph{Physical interpretation.} Each tri-octonion state \(v\) can be interpreted as a hadron, where the three imaginary octonions represent the three confined quarks within it. The physical dynamics and interactions of the hadron are governed by the associator \([v]\), which encodes the intrinsic non-associative interaction tension binding the quarks.
    \item \emph{Inter-hadron interactions via associator commutator.} For two such states \(v_1, v_2\), define the commutator of associators:
    \[
    [[v_1],[v_2]] := [v_1][v_2] - [v_2][v_1] = 2\, [v_1] \times [v_2],
    \]
    capturing the algebraic interplay of hadron interactions through the octonionic cross product.
    \item \emph{Almost-complex structure on \(S^6\).} The almost-complex structure \(J\) at a point on the 6-sphere \(S^6\) is defined by
    \[
    J_g(v) = g \times v,
    \]
    where \(g \in \operatorname{Im}(\mathbb{O})\) is an imaginary unit octonion specifying a direction on the 6-sphere, and \(\times\) denotes the octonionic cross product. This aligns with the natural almost-complex structure arising from octonion multiplication and encodes the internal geometry and topological properties of hadronic interactions within the AoO framework.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Nijenhuis Tensor and Non-Integrability}

An almost–complex structure \(J\) on a manifold \(M\) is a bundle map \(J: TM \to TM\) with \(J^2 = -\mathrm{Id}\).

The classical test for integrability is the vanishing of the \emph{Nijenhuis tensor}:
\[
N_J(X,Y) = [JX, JY] - J[JX, Y] - J[X, JY] - [X,Y],
\]
for vector fields \(X,Y\) on \(M\) \cite{KobNom1969}. 

If \(N_J = 0\), the almost-complex structure \(J\) is integrable, i.e., arises from local holomorphic coordinates and \(M\) is a complex manifold.

For the octonionic \(S^6\), the almost–complex structure \(J_g(v) = g \times v\) has \(N_J \neq 0\), proving that \(S^6\) cannot admit a global integrable complex structure.

Within AoO, this geometric obstruction is reinterpreted physically: the unavoidable “torsion” measured by \(N_J\) corresponds to electroweak mass generation, linking geometric and algebraic obstacles directly to particle physics.

\section{Arithmetic Obstruction: Brauer–Manin from \(x^2+1=0\)}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Local solvability over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\).} The equation \(x^2 + 1 = 0\) has solutions over \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) if and only if \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) or \(p=2\). It fails for primes with \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\).
    \item \emph{Rational octonion algebra splitting.} The rational octonion algebra \(B\), satisfying
    \[
    B \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{R} \cong \mathbb{O},
    \]
    splits exactly at primes where \(x^2+1=0\) is solvable, but remains a division algebra at “bad” primes.
    \item \emph{Brauer–Manin obstruction.} For primes \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\), no local imaginary unit “\(i\)” exists; by the Hasse principle, this yields a global obstruction forbidding a global integrable complex structure on \(S^6\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Unified Physical Law: Mass as Arithmetic Inevitability}

The geometric obstruction \(N_J \neq 0\) and the arithmetic Brauer–Manin obstruction (division primes for \(x^2+1=0\)) are two facets of the same fundamental law.

AoO interprets this law physically: massless electroweak propagation requires a global complex structure on \(S^6\)—which number theory forbids. Consequently, the \(W\) and \(Z\) bosons inevitably acquire mass. Mass here is not contingent but \emph{arithmetically mandated}.
